# Source notes: "The science of iterations"

Keynote by Spencer (agency owner, founder of the Evolve community), Commerce Roundtable, 2026,
about 45 minutes including Q&A. The full Swedish notes are in the Claude Doc "Annonsiterationer –
anteckningar från Spencer (Evolve)" (https://claude.ai/artifact/B9Mfg2KZsMwC9vKNmeC13p). Timestamps
below point into the video.

## Results that frame the talk (0:00–4:23)
- After Meta's Andromeda update many think iterations no longer work. He turned his iteration process
  into a playbook six months earlier.
- A telehealth brand: $30k/month at $600 CPA in February → $2.5M/month at $245 CPA. That is 86x spend,
  59 % lower CPA and 211x new customers a month, with creative only.
- Community members doing $100k+/day; one doing $5M/month by cracking unique mechanisms and formats.
  "They're not smarter than you. They learned to stop guessing."
- Plan: 1 Label, 2 Learnings, 3 Iterate, 4 Playbook.

## Label (4:23–8:51)
- Winners exist on a scale. Based on $160M ad spend this year (about $100M last year).
- Four variables: KPI, spend, scale (budget increase), time.
- Four boxes: Loser, KPI winner ("I look at these as losers"), Spend winner, Breakthrough ("all I
  care about"). "Each box tells you exactly how to iterate."
- The 10–30 % threshold came from a custom Python script run over four months. Smaller accounts give
  winners a bigger share. The difference between a $50k and a $1M account is the number of
  breakthroughs. Benchmarks by account size are in `labeling.md`.
- Naming: track the batch and the iteration at minimum (`Iter#2-Batch#3`), so you can ask "how many
  iterations have we made on our breakthroughs?" "Two." "Let's do way more."

## Learnings (8:51–14:00)
- Volume vs intent: both work. Creators are the volume engine; the creative team is the intent engine.
  More ads only matter if they make more breakthroughs.
- His team: 7 editors, 2 creative strategists, 1 growth strategist, 300 ads a month, all with a memo.
  One strategist and one editor making 300 ads a month probably lack intent.
- The memo: what is the ad, why are we doing it, how will we do it.
- Surface learnings ("it's UGC", "great hook", "low CPC") lead to surface iterations, "and Andromeda
  doesn't like that".
- Nine components: angle, avatar, desire, mechanism, authority (highlighted), awareness level,
  emotion (valence and intensity), positioning, urgency. Authority example: an iteration with an
  authority switch took $15k/day and helped a member go from $50k to $100k/day in a week.

## Worked breakdown (14:00–21:47)
A washing machine cleaner tablet ad (pet owners, smelly laundry). The full component table is at the
end of `components.md`. Key points:
- Found with an ad spy tool sorted by shares. "99.9 % confident this is a breakthrough."
- Authority in the hook: "My grandpa used to fix washing machines for a living." Who do you believe
  more, a brand or that grandpa? He sniffs once and names the problem; he recommends the mechanism,
  not the brand.
- Avatar by experiences, behaviors, emotions; "I don't care that she's 42 and lives in California,
  only that she has a dog."
- Solution aware because it lists tried solutions instead of agitating.
- Urgency: "You're not washing your clothes, you're slow cooking them in mold."
- Mechanism: 3 enzymes, each tied to what it does. Explaining the mechanism took the telehealth brand
  from about $3k to $30k/day.
- Positioning: "first and only enzyme-based cleaning tablet". Be different, not better (red powder
  among green powders).

## Iterations (21:47–27:35)
- Definition: "a new ad that inherits a learning from an existing ad, usually to improve a result."
- Iteration 1 changed avatar (broad), desire (status: co-workers notice the smell) and awareness
  (problem aware: "this will be uncomfortable to hear"). It inherited the grandpa and became another
  breakthrough. Urgency became "people are realizing how nasty their machines are, so it's selling
  out". Tie sales urgency to the ad.
- Iteration 2 changed angle (keep the machine clean and mold-free), awareness (unaware: "just add
  'did you know'"), emotion (high intensity) and authority (the "3 common mistakes" list format
  creates authority bias). It inherited the mold urgency and moved it to the front. It is the brand's
  top ad. He would still add the grandpa; the brand has that version running too.
- Copy tip: invert "3 best ways" into "3 common mistakes" or "3 red flags".

## Formats, playbook and quick win (27:35–32:06)
- Easiest win: put the best ad into other formats (UGC → AI-animated → podcast → AI voiceover).
  Creative diversification is concepts and formats.
- Big four when nine is too much: angle, authority, unique mechanism, urgency.
- Quick win: take your best ad from the last 30 days; label it (share of spend, budget increase since
  launch); spend 20–30 minutes finding the big four; if one is missing add it, otherwise add or switch
  authority, add urgency, make the mechanism clearer or more unique; make another ad with intent.
- For founders: a Slack channel with the creative team, give them the resource, and a report every
  Friday on what they learned and how it will be actioned.

## Q&A (32:06–44:53)
- **CBO or ABO:** everything in CBO; preference. A testing KPI winner is a breakthrough once it pulls
  spend in scaling.
- **Who does learnings:** everyone. Two one-hour calls a week. Whoever made the ad does its learnings
  (talk it out with a dictation tool, let Claude organize it). Include editors. Media buyers bring the
  numbers ("hook rate is great, what do you think?").
- **Landing pages per iteration:** page congruency matters. Evergreen page for everything; a dedicated
  page once an angle works.
- **When is it a new concept:** rarely one winning component; changing only one can break the ad.
  Comfortable changing several.
- **Creators:** they are their own strategists; let them do learnings. Volume and intent engines work
  together.
- **AI ads:** fine for animation and B-roll; wary of AI humans; Meta may label AI, which could boost
  real UGC.
- **From winner to page:** depends on the control page; click-focused ads → advertorial or listicle;
  complete ads → simpler page; trial and error; offer is massive; start with headline congruency
  (first screen calls out the exact problem).
- **Hook testing under $50k/month:** always. One concept, three hooks, each analysed by component.
- **First breakthrough:** test many angles broadly, then go deep on the one that works. Imitation ads:
  run learnings on competitors' top ads and iterate on them.
