# Iteration recipes

Contents: Pick by box · Component recipes · Format swaps · Hook tests · Imitation ads and the first
breakthrough · Landing pages · Volume and intent · Iteration or new concept?

## Pick by box
| Box | Diagnosis | Keep | Change first |
|---|---|---|---|
| Breakthrough | It works | Almost everything | Authority, desire, awareness or format, one batch at a time. Make many. |
| KPI winner | Sells, but Meta won't spend on it | Angle, mechanism, offer, authority | Hook, format, lower awareness, higher intensity, broader avatar |
| Spend winner | Gets reach, doesn't sell | Hook, format, opening | Authority, mechanism clarity, positioning, offer, landing page match |
| Loser | Nothing proven | Nothing, or one strong line | New concept, or an imitation ad |

## Component recipes
Each recipe says what to inherit and what to change. Combine two or three when the ad needs it.

**Authority switch.** Keep the story beats and change who tells them or what proves them: expert →
other expert, expert → demonstration, voice → list format, add a third person to an ad that has none.
Most reliable lever. The authority must be true or clearly a story.

**Add the authority to a format ad.** A list or educational ad already has format authority. Adding a
person on top ("my grandpa, a washing machine repairman, says the three biggest mistakes are…") is
often the next winner.

**Awareness down.** Start a solution- or problem-aware winner with a "Did you know…?" line or a
startling fact, and drop the assumption that the viewer knows the problem. Broadens the audience.

**Awareness up.** Start by agitating ("If you've never cleaned your …, this will be uncomfortable to
hear") or by listing what they've tried. Narrows the audience and deepens the match.

**Desire switch.** Keep the problem and the product, change what is at stake: health → status (what
others notice), → money (not having to replace something), → comfort (no scrubbing), → family (the
kids' clothes).

**Intensity up.** Same body, more intense hook: a vivid image or consequence instead of a mild
description. Also try the reverse when an intense ad tires.

**Angle switch.** Promise a different outcome of the same product. Usually needs new opening lines
and a matching landing page headline.

**Urgency to the front.** Take the strongest urgency or consequence line from inside the ad and make
it the first sentence.

**Story-tied urgency.** Replace a generic "sale ends" with a reason rooted in the ad's story. Only if
true.

**Inversion.** Turn "3 best ways to…" into "3 common mistakes", "3 red flags", "stop doing this".
People may not need to be the best, but they don't want to get it wrong. Holds attention to the end.

**Mechanism to benefit.** Rewrite each part of the mechanism as ingredient + action + benefit, or make
a short ad that only explains how the product works.

**Positioning by difference.** Replace "better than" claims with what makes it different: who it is
made for, what it is made of, the enemy it replaces.

**Avatar broadening or narrowing.** Broaden with less aware openings; narrow with a specific
experience in line one ("Do you have pets and towels that smell like wet dog?").

## Format swaps
"Often you can inherit everything and just change the visuals." Format is one of the cheapest ways to
diversify, because Meta reads concepts *and* formats. Take the best ad and put the same script into:
- UGC talking head or voiceover over B-roll
- AI-animated explainer (clearly animated is fine)
- Podcast clip (two people discussing it)
- AI voiceover over stock or generated B-roll
- List or education ("3 mistakes"), whiteboard or text-on-screen
- Demonstration, test or teardown
- Static image or carousel (offer ads: see the `bof-offer-ads` skill)

Avoid AI people presented as real people.

## Hook tests
- One concept (the overall idea of the ad) with three hooks.
- Make the three hooks lead with different components, for example authority-led, urgency-led and an
  unaware "Did you know…?" hook.
- When a hook wins, ask what component made it win and carry that into the next concept.
- Small accounts should still test hooks. Big and small accounts run the same program; only the speed
  of testing and the budget differ.

## Imitation ads and the first breakthrough
- Getting from zero to the first breakthrough is the hardest stage.
- **Broad, then deep.** First test many different angles to find a direction. When one angle works,
  run learnings and go deep: make the best possible version and its iterations.
- **Imitation ads:** take a competitor's top ad, run the learnings on it ("that's their authority,
  that's their angle"), and make an iteration of it for your product. Don't name the competitor.

## Landing pages
- Match the page to the ad. An ad about smelly laundry should land on a page that talks about smelly
  laundry, at least on the first screen.
- Default setup: one evergreen page for all ads. When an angle starts working, build a dedicated page
  for it.
- Ask what the viewer needs after this ad. A click-bait style ad needs an advertorial or listicle to do
  the selling. An ad that already covers everything may do better with a simple product page.
- The easiest start is the headline and first screen: repeat the ad's call-out word for word.
- The offer matters as much as the copy.
- Pages are trial and error like ads. One failed page doesn't prove pages don't work.

## Volume and intent
- **Volume engine:** creators and freelancers who make many ads with freedom.
- **Intent engine:** the creative team that makes ads with a memo (what, why, how) from learnings.
- They feed each other: the intent engine tells the volume engine "this angle works, this authority
  works, go".
- More ads only help if they produce more breakthroughs. Optimize for breakthroughs, not ad count.

## Iteration or new concept?
There is rarely one component that wins on its own. Changing only one can break the ad's flow, so
change several when needed. It is still an iteration as long as it consciously inherits a learning.
The more ads with a component win, the more proof that the component drives results.
